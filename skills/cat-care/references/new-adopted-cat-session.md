# Newly Adopted Cat Adaptation Session Notes

This reference captures useful patterns from a session where the user had just adopted a cat from another household. The cat was initially hiding upstairs, then began exploring, vocalizing, rubbing against the user's hand, and encountering household hazards.

## Context pattern

- Cat was on day 3 in the new home.
- Home has two floors; user mostly stayed on first floor while cat stayed on second floor.
- Cat hid at first, then began running around upstairs, playing with curtains, biting data cables, coming downstairs, meowing sharply/at length, and eventually rubbing the user's hand/arm while making a purring-like sound.

## Advice that worked well

- Reassure that hiding for several days is common if eating/drinking/litter use continue.
- Treat the upstairs as a safe zone at first; keep food/water/litter/hiding places stable.
- Human should visit calmly for short periods, speak softly, avoid staring/reaching, and bring treats/food.
- If cat approaches but runs when user moves, frame it as “wants interaction but still lacks full trust.” Recommend sitting/low posture, slow movements, slow blinking, treats, and no forced touching.
- Long sharp meows after rehoming can mean the cat is searching for familiar people/place/scent and seeking reassurance. Emphasize predictable new-home routines.
- Head rubbing against the user's hand/arm is a strong positive sign: scent marking and trust-building. Recommend gentle chin/cheek/ear scratches only if the cat continues to solicit contact.

## Behavior interpretations used

- Low tail sweeping left-right: emotional arousal; slow may be cautious/uncertain, fast may mean irritation.
- Purring/“motor” sound: comfort or self-soothing; interpret with body language.
- Chattering/clicking while focused on a target: hunting excitement.
- Playing with curtains: exploration/hunting/climbing, but manage risks.
- Biting cables: common but dangerous; use physical prevention, not scolding.

## Household hazards highlighted

- Cables: risk of chewing damage, electric shock, burns, short circuit, and swallowed plastic. Use cable sleeves/boxes/ties; stop using damaged cables.
- Curtains/window area: secure windows/screens, tie up cords, prevent claw entrapment or rods falling.
- Coffee grounds/liquid/residue: caffeine risk for cats; clean and block access. Observe for 6–12h after minor licking, especially agitation, fast heartbeat, tremors, vomiting/diarrhea, drooling, seizures.
- Ordinary milk: not recommended due to lactose intolerance; small accidental licks usually less urgent than caffeine but prevent recurrence.

## Medical red flags repeated

- No eating for >24h.
- No drinking for >24–36h.
- No urine/stool for around 48h.
- Repeated vomiting/diarrhea, lethargy, labored breathing, drooling, tremors, seizures, collapse.
- Suspected significant caffeine ingestion or swallowed wire/plastic/string-like material with symptoms.
