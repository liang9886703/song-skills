# Kitten weight and deworming session notes

Context: user has a newly adopted kitten described as 4 months old, golden point, measured at 0.7 kg about one week after arrival. Prior caregiver said deworming was done before arrival, but medication/date/dose details were not available in the chat.

Reusable guidance distilled from session:

- 0.7 kg at 4 months is light for many kittens, including British/golden-point type lines. Avoid panic if energy/appetite/stool are normal, but do not dismiss it as automatically normal.
- Recommend weekly weigh-ins at the same time/context. For kittens, stable upward trend matters; flat or declining weight warrants vet assessment.
- Ask for prior deworming details: product name, date, internal vs external coverage, dose, and whether it was weight-based.
- If prior deworming is vague and kitten is very light, avoid immediate repeat dosing casually; dosing margins are small and products have minimum weight/age limits.
- Best next step for the first deworming under user's care: pet hospital/vet clinic, ideally with basic exam + fecal test + weight-based plan. After product/dose is clarified and kitten is stable, future routine prevention can often be done at home with legitimate products.
- Pet shop is less preferred for underweight kittens or uncertain medical history because the core need is medical judgment, not just applying a spot-on or selling a tablet.

Suggested concise answer pattern:

> 这次建议先去宠物医院，不优先宠物店。因为它 4 个月但 0.7kg，剂量和最低体重限制比较关键；医院能称重、粪检、确认体内/体外方案。后续等身体稳定、药和剂量明确后，可以自己买正规药在家做。
